## Placeholders and tags

Parts shown in code format are filled in or used by the site. Keep them exactly as they are; you may move them within the sentence, as Turkish word order needs.

- `{date}`, `{count}`, `{name}` and the like: a value the site fills in (a date, a number, a name, a web address).
- `<link>`…`</link>`, and likewise `<email>`, `<privacy>`, `<kvkk>` and `<strong>`: the words between the two tags become a link (or bold text). Translate the words and keep both tags around them.
- `{count, plural, =0 {…} one {…} other {…}}`: chooses the words by a number. `#` stands for the number; `=0` is used for zero, `one` for 1 and `other` for any other number. Translate only the words inside the inner braces, and keep every branch, even where Turkish uses the same words for 1 and for more.
- An apostrophe (`'`) directly before `{`, `}` or `#` turns them into plain text; use `’` there instead.

## Turkish text from other sources

The interface also shows Turkish text that is not in the messages. None of it is written by hand in the repository, so none of it is listed above:

- Country names come from the Unicode CLDR (version 48, as built into Node.js) and are stored unchanged in the seed data (`src/migrations/data/geography-2026-09.ts`). Not shown yet: countries appear only on English pages.
- Dates (such as the date at the end of the Turkish legal pages), numbers, language names (the original language of a job) and time-zone names are written in the page's language by the standard formatting of the server and browser (`Intl`, from the CLDR).
- The taxonomy (subfields, methods, model systems, position types, career stages) and the country sets are in English only so far; their Turkish labels are entered in the admin with the Turkish site (PLAN §12).
