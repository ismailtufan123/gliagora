### Glia Notes newsletter

Glia Notes is the monthly Gliagora newsletter. The first issue comes with our public launch. Until then, if you sign up on the newsletter page, you receive only the confirmation email and a welcome email; if you switch Glia Notes on in your account settings, you receive nothing until the first issue.

- **What:** your email address and that you are subscribed. No name.
- **Where it comes from:** you. You sign up on the [newsletter page](/en/newsletter/). Members can also switch Glia Notes on in their account settings.
- **Why:** to send you Glia Notes once a month.
- **Legal basis:** your explicit consent (KVKK Art. 5(1); GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)).
- **How long:** until you unsubscribe. When you unsubscribe with the link in an email or your mail program's unsubscribe button, we delete your address from the newsletter list at once; when you switch Glia Notes off in your account settings, within minutes (usually at once). A signup you never confirm is deleted after 7 days.

How signup works:

1. You enter your email address and tick the box "I agree to receive Glia Notes by email." The box is never ticked in advance.
2. We send you a confirmation email. The link in it is valid for 7 days. You can ask for a new one after 60 seconds; an earlier link keeps working until one of them is used.
3. Your subscription starts only when you open the link and press the button on the page it opens. We then send you a welcome email, which says how to unsubscribe.

Until you confirm, your signup is only its confirmation link, kept in our main database: your address, the language of the page, the version of the consent text you agreed to and the time you sent the form. It is not in the newsletter software and not in our backups, and it is deleted after 7 days. So that the "Check your inbox" page can show your address (partly hidden) and send a new link, your browser keeps a cookie for one hour (see "Cookies and browser storage").

Only confirmed subscribers are stored in our newsletter software (listmonk) on our server in Türkiye: the address, with no name and without your IP address. Once you confirm, our main database no longer keeps your address for the newsletter (your confirmation links are deleted then): only listmonk's number and random code (UUID) for your subscription, in the consent records (see below) and, if you are a member, on your account, where your account's address is kept too (see "Member accounts"). Glia Notes is sent from digest@gliagora.com through our mail provider in Türkiye.

If you are a member, the email address you verified at sign-in counts as the confirmation: when you switch Glia Notes on in your settings, your account's address is subscribed within minutes, and when you change your account's address, the subscription moves to the new one. If your account's address already receives Glia Notes through the newsletter page when you create the account or add that address, your account takes that subscription over within minutes: your settings show Glia Notes on, and its consent records stay as the newsletter page recorded them. ### Spam protection on forms

- **ALTCHA:** before a form is sent, your browser solves a small puzzle. This shows that a person, not a spam program, is sending the form. ALTCHA comes from our own server and is checked there; no outside service is involved.
- **Hidden field:** forms contain a field that people do not see and spam programs often fill in.
- **Rate limits:** each form accepts only a limited number of sends from the same IP address. For example, the suggestion form, the "Report a problem" form and the data-request form each accept at most 5 an hour; the sign-in form and the other forms are limited too. Your IP address is the number that identifies your internet connection. For these limits we store a scrambled code made from your IP address (a hash).
- **Limits per email address:** the forms that send an email (sign-in links, links to add an address to an account, and the data-request form) are also limited per email address, so that nobody can fill someone's inbox. We store a scrambled code (a hash) made from the address written in one standard way: in lower case, without a tag such as `name+news@`, and, for a Gmail address, without dots. Different spellings of one mailbox are then counted as one address. The site also sends at most a fixed number of such emails per hour in all. The form that adds or changes the address of an account also counts the links each account asked for, by the account's number.
- **Where it comes from:** automatically, by electronic means, when your browser sends a form.
- **Legal basis:** our legitimate interest in protecting the forms, and the inboxes they send to, from abuse (KVKK Art. 5(2)(f); GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).
- **How long:** the codes and counts are kept only in the website's database on our server and deleted after 24 hours. These codes and counts are personal data too.

### Backups

- **What:** encrypted copies of the databases described above: the website's and the newsletter software's. Links that are waiting to be used (sign-in, confirmation and newsletter signup links) and the codes of the rate limits are left out.
- **Where it comes from:** copied automatically from our databases.
- **Where:** encrypted copies are kept on our server and, every night, copied to a second server at another company in Türkiye, not our main hosting company.
- **Why:** to restore the service after a failure.
- **Legal basis:** the same basis as the original data, and our legitimate interest in keeping data safe (KVKK Art. 5(2)(f); GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).
- **How long:** we keep one copy for each of the last 7 days, the last 4 weeks and the last 6 months, plus every copy from the last 2 days. So anything you delete, including a deleted account and the address of a former subscriber, disappears from the backups within 6 months.
- **Deletions survive a restore:** when we delete data because someone asked us to (a data request, a deleted account) or because an account was inactive, we note the number of each deleted record, with no personal data, for 7 months. So we do for each address deleted from the newsletter software (by listmonk's number for it) and each consent record we unlink. ## How we protect your data

- **Location:** your data is stored on servers we rent in Türkiye. The database cannot be reached from the internet, and each program on the server can reach only its own database.
- **Connections:** all connections to the site are encrypted (HTTPS), and browsers are told to always use the encrypted connection (HSTS).
- **Pages:** our pages tell browsers not to load anything from other websites (Content Security Policy). Other sites receive at most our domain name as the referring site. Our pages cannot use your camera, microphone or location, and the map never asks for your location.
- **Member sign-in:** no passwords. Sign-in links work once and expire after 15 minutes. Each emailed link can be used only for what it was sent for (signing in, adding an address, confirming a request), and a sign-in link opened in another browser than the one that asked for it shows a warning first. Sign-in with Google and ORCID uses standard checks that stop others from taking over the sign-in (state, a one-time value in the provider's reply (nonce) and, where the provider supports it, PKCE). A new session starts at each sign-in.
- **Editor access:** a long passphrase, a second sign-in step, a 15-minute lockout after 5 failed attempts, and an audit log. Members cannot reach the editing tools.
- **Forms:** all input is checked on the server. Spam protection and rate limits apply.
- **Server:** automatic security updates; administrators log in to the server with a key file, never a password; a firewall; blocking of repeated failed logins; and programs that run without administrator rights.
- **Software:** the software packages we build on are fixed to known versions, scanned for known security problems and checked for updates every week.
- **Backups:** encrypted, stored at a second location in Türkiye and tested every month.
- **Incidents:** we follow a written plan to contain and assess an incident. If a breach affects personal data, we notify the Personal Data Protection Board, where the law requires it, within 72 hours of finding out about the breach. [TO DECIDE: B15 – breach notification rule and informing the people affected]
- **Working practices:** test systems use invented data only. We do not put data about our users into AI tools, issue trackers or screenshots.
