## About this page

Gliagora | Neuroscience collects positions in neuroscience, and will add events, funding, papers, news and resources step by step. This page explains where our items come from, how we check and write them, what the site does by itself, and how to get something corrected. It is the longer version of "How curation works" on the [About page](/en/about/).

## What an item is

Every item on Gliagora has three parts:

- **the key facts**, such as the employer, the place, the type of position and the deadline, each checked on the original page;
- **a short summary in our own words**;
- **a link to the original page**, where you apply, register or read on.

We do not copy descriptions, abstracts, images or logos. We do not take applications, charge fees or act between you and an employer. The [terms of use](/en/legal/terms/) say more about this.

## Where items come from

| Source | What we use it for | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| Employers' own pages: universities, research institutes and companies | Positions | Every listing rests on the employer's own page, and links to it. Our program reads that page once more just before the listing is published. |
| Job boards, the boards of scientific societies, mailing lists and social media | Finding positions | Only to learn that a position exists. We then look for it on the employer's own page and link there. We publish nothing from a board's own listing, and we never copy a board in bulk. |
| Your suggestions ([send us one](/en/suggest/)) | Positions | Each suggestion is checked against the original page. A suggestion is never published automatically: only in a publishing round that our editor starts. |
| The Research Organization Registry (ROR) | The names, places and identifiers of institutions | Taken from ROR's public data, which is free to reuse (CC0). |
| OpenStreetMap | The map, and the time zone in which a deadline ends | See "Data and credits" on the [About page](/en/about/). |

We list only positions that employers advertise themselves. We do not list advertisements placed by recruitment agencies.

## How we check

Positions are published in publishing rounds that our editor starts. In each round, an AI assistant checks every waiting position against our written rules, and our program reads the original page once more just before a position is published. A person does not check every position; positions the rules leave uncertain wait for our editor. [TO DECIDE: B17 – whether each listing must also say that it was chosen and summarised with an AI assistant] A position is published only when:

- it is in neuroscience, and it is a research or research-support position at a research employer;
- the original page can be reached, it is the employer's own, and it does not say that the position is filled or closed;
- its deadline is at least three days away, or it is open until filled and was published in the last two months;
- it is listed at the institution where the work happens;
- its facts (the type of position, the deadline, the place and the subfields) agree with the original posting;
- the summary is our own, with no copied sentences;
- nothing we show names a person or gives an email address or a phone number: you contact the employer through the original page;
- there are no logos or images.

Our editor may decide otherwise for a single position, for example to publish one whose deadline is less than three days away or one that is open until filled but older. Our editor never publishes a position whose deadline has passed, whose page is gone or says it is closed, or whose listing would name a person or give an email address or a phone number.

We publish a deadline or a condition only after we have seen it on the original page. Each item shows the date we last verified it: the day our program read the page before publishing it, or the day our editor last checked or corrected it. A position leaves the lists by itself when its deadline has passed. The deadline day ends in the time zone of the institution, unless the posting names another.

If our item and the original page differ, the original page is correct. Please tell us, so that we can correct the item.

Items sometimes name people, for example the head of a lab, as the original page does. The [privacy policy](/en/legal/privacy/#people-named-in-listings-and-sources) explains how we handle those names.

## What the site does by itself

Our editor starts every publishing round and is responsible for every item. The site does these things by itself:

- it takes a position out of the lists when its deadline has passed;
- it brings in the public list of research institutions from ROR;
- it sends the emails that answer a form or a request;
- it reads some employers' own vacancy pages and feeds once a day, and puts the new research positions in neuroscience into our queue;
- once a week it checks that the original page of every published position still answers; in the next publishing round, positions whose page is gone or says that the position is filled are taken offline.

Our rule for sources: a program may read a source by itself only if the source is an official feed or interface, or the vacancy pages of an employer (a university, institute or company), and its terms of use and its `robots.txt` file allow it. A person reads everything else. Job boards are never read by a program or copied in bulk, whatever their terms say.

Our program names itself "Gliagora-Monitor" and links to the section "Reading employers' pages" on the [About page](/en/about/). It reads each source at most once a day, makes one request at a time to a site, checks `robots.txt` before every run and leaves alone any site whose terms of use forbid automated reading; for each source we note the address of its terms and when we read them and its `robots.txt`. It keeps only what our editor needs to review a position: its address, its title, the facts we check, such as the deadline, and its text with names, email addresses and phone numbers removed, until 30 days after the editor's decision. It never publishes anything by itself: the positions it finds are published only in a publishing round that our editor starts, after an AI assistant has checked each one against our written rules and the program has read the employer's page once more. ## AI tools

An AI assistant helps us choose and summarise positions. [TO DECIDE: B17 – transfer route for AI drafting, and whether each listing must say that it was written with an AI assistant] It works like this:

- when our editor starts a publishing round, Claude Code, Anthropic's AI assistant, used by our technical administrator, prepares a draft for each new position: a short summary in our own words, facts such as the deadline and the place, and tags, each saying what it came from. It then checks each position against our written rules (see "How we check"): it publishes those that meet every rule, turns down those that clearly do not, and leaves the uncertain ones for our editor. The Claude API, also by Anthropic, could do the same each day; it is switched off;
- before any text is sent, email addresses, phone numbers, web addresses and personal names are replaced with placeholders. The rules that find names are not perfect, so a name can still be sent. For papers, once that section opens, the author lists and contact details are removed;
- nothing is published without our editor starting the round, and a person does not check every position before it is published. Our editor is responsible for every listing and corrects or removes any of them as soon as a problem is found. Report a problem with any listing and we look at it at once.

The introduction at the top of each subfield page may also start from a first draft written with the same AI assistant, from the names and descriptions of our subfields alone. That draft is never shown on the site either: our editor writes the introduction you read, checks it and approves it, and is responsible for it, as for every item.

The [privacy policy](/en/legal/privacy/) explains what would be sent and where.

## Corrections

If you find a mistake, tell us. Every item has a "Report a problem" link, and the [notice and takedown page](/en/legal/takedown/) lists every way to reach us and how quickly we act.

- We correct a fact as soon as we have checked it on the original page. The item's "Last verified" date then shows that day.
- We take a listing offline at once when the position is withdrawn.
- We keep a record of changes and of requests, as the notice and takedown page describes.

## Employers

Employers cannot edit items themselves. Nobody needs our permission to be listed, and nobody pays for it. If you write for an employer:

- **To change a fact,** update your own page first, then tell us. We change the item once we have seen the change there.
- **To withdraw a position,** tell us, and we take the listing offline at once.
- **To suggest a position,** [send it to us](/en/suggest/).
- **If you do not want a position listed,** ask us to remove the listing and tell us why.

The [notice and takedown page](/en/legal/takedown/#employers-updates-and-removals) has the details.

## Independence, payment and sponsorship

- No one pays to appear on Gliagora, and no one can pay for a better place in a list. Lists are ordered by deadline or by date, and search results by how well they match your words.
- Gliagora takes no money from the employers, funders, publishers or organisers it lists. At present, it carries no advertising or sponsorship.
- If we ever add sponsorship, we will label sponsored content clearly. It will not change which items we list or how we describe them, and we will update this page and the terms of use first.
